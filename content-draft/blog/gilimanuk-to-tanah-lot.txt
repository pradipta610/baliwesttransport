## 1. Verified Fact Base (cross-checked across multiple sources, July 2026)

**Distance and drive time:**
- Straight private-car distance Gilimanuk → Tanah Lot area (Tabanan Regency): **~110–115 km**. Cross-check points: Gilimanuk–Tabanan town is ~105 km / 65 miles (Rome2Rio), and Tanah Lot sits roughly 15–20 km further south/west of Tabanan town, off the same main highway.
- **This is Bali West Transport's shortest and fastest Gilimanuk transfer route** — genuinely differentiating fact, use it. Every other built route (Ubud 127 km, Canggu 148 km, Seminyak 125 km, Kuta 133 km, Sanur 130 km, plus the new Uluwatu/Nusa Dua routes at ~145–148 km) is longer.
- Why it's shorter: Tanah Lot sits directly along Bali's main Denpasar–Gilimanuk highway corridor (via Negara–Tabanan), so the route doesn't need to detour into Denpasar city traffic or climb into the Bukit Peninsula the way Uluwatu/Nusa Dua/Kuta/Canggu routes do.
- **Realistic private car travel time: approximately 2.5 to 3.5 hours**, depending on departure time and traffic around Tabanan. (Best-case optimistic estimates from some route calculators quote 1.5–2 hours; treat those as best-case only — real-world Bali road conditions, ferry disembarkation, and traffic through Tabanan township make 2.5–3.5 hours the realistic range to publish. This mirrors how BWT's own live "Gilimanuk to Ubud" blog post already handles the same optimistic-vs-realistic gap: it quotes 127 km but 3.5–5 hours realistic, well above simple distance-calculator estimates.)
- Early-morning departures (before 8 AM) are meaingfully faster than midday departures, which hit Tabanan market traffic.

**Tanah Lot Temple facts (verified against tanahlot.org, official-adjacent sources, and multiple 2026-dated guides — consistent across sources):**
- Full name: Pura Tanah Lot ("Land in the Sea" temple)
- Location: Beraban village, Kediri district, Tabanan Regency, Bali
- Built: 16th century, founded by the Hindu priest Dang Hyang Nirartha (Dang Hyang Dwijendra)
- One of Bali's Sad Kahyangan (six directional sea temples)
- Opening hours: **07:00–19:00 daily**, no scheduled closures except Nyepi (Balinese Day of Silence)
- Entrance fee (foreign visitors, as of 2026): **IDR 75,000 adults / IDR 40,000 children** — cash only, paid at the gate, no online booking
- Parking: IDR 5,000 (car) / IDR 2,000–3,000 (motorcycle) — separate from entrance fee
- Optional Kecak Fire Dance performance: nightly at ~18:00, separate ticket (~IDR 100,000) — mention as optional, not bundled
- Access to the temple base depends on tide — low tide exposes a walkable path to the rock; at high tide it's cut off and only viewable from the cliffs. Recommend checking tide times before finalizing sunset plans.
- Visitors cannot enter the inner shrine (reserved for Balinese Hindu worshippers) but can access temple grounds, coastal walkways, and viewpoints
- Recommended visit duration: 1.5–2 hours
- Best time to visit: arrive by ~4:00 PM to secure a sunset viewing spot before the ~6:15–6:45 PM sunset; mornings (7–9 AM) are quieter with fewer crowds
- **Note for the page:** state that fees/hours are accurate as of 2026 but subject to change, and suggest confirming at the gate — this kind of calibrated accuracy (not overclaiming) is itself a positive trust signal for AI citation.
